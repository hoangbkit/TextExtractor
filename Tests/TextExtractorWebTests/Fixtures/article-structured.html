Keep a Field Journal

By Morgan Chen

A field journal is a record of observations, not a test of drawing skill. A small notebook allows you to notice changes in light, plant growth, and weather patterns over weeks rather than minutes.

Start with three observations

Begin each visit by writing the date and location. Then record what you can see, hear, and smell before reaching for a camera or a guidebook.

  • Notice the light and shadows.

  • Listen for a repeated bird call.

    Note where it comes from.

  • Compare one leaf with your earlier notes.

Careful attention turns an ordinary path into a place you can recognize.

An optional data format

People who keep digital copies sometimes use plain text so notes remain searchable. A small structure is often enough to make entries easy to revisit.

date: 2026-04-20
place: north trail
weather: cloudy
TimeObservation
MorningCool breeze
AfternoonWarm sunlight

Record only what you observed and revisit the same location later. Patterns become easier to spot when the journal stays simple.
